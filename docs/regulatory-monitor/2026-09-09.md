## 🔴 Finding 1 — Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs (RATE DATABASE GAP)

### What changed
Section 122's 150-day statutory maximum expired at midnight **July 24, 2026**. The Administration immediately replaced it with new tariffs under **Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2411)** targeting forced-labor trade practices.

### Rates
| Tier | Rate | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Partial compliance | **10%** | Countries with a forced-labor import prohibition in place, OR committed to enact one |
| Non-compliant | **12.5%** | All other covered economies with no meaningful enforcement |

### 10% countries (partial compliance)
Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad and Tobago, United Kingdom.

### Exclusions
- USMCA-compliant goods (Canada, Mexico)
- CAFTA-DR textile and apparel (six Central American/Caribbean nations)
- In-transit goods loaded before 12:01 AM Jul 24 and entered before 12:01 AM Jul 28, 2026

### HTS codes
9903.05.96 through 9903.06.21 (country-specific within that range)

### Legal authority
Section 301, Trade Act of 1974. USTR investigation into 60 economies. Presidential action signed July 23, 2026.

### Calculator impact
Our seed file had `S122-01.supersededBy = null` and no Section 301 entries. Gap since July 24 — **46 days of missing rate coverage**.

## 🟡 Finding 2 — CAPE Phase 2 Launch (Jun 29, 2026)

### What changed
CBP launched CAPE Phase 2 on **June 29, 2026**. Phase 2 expands CAPE to accept entries of type 01, 02, and 06 that are **flagged for reconciliation** — provided the Type 09 reconciliation entry has NOT yet been filed. Phase 1 (Apr 20, 2026) did not explicitly cover reconciliation-flagged entries.

### Critical filing sequence
CAPE declaration must be filed BEFORE the Type 09 reconciliation entry. Filing Type 09 first permanently locks the underlying entry out of CAPE Phase 2.

### Still excluded
Type 09 (filed reconciliation entry), Type 08, Type 23, and Type 47 (drawback) remain excluded from CAPE in both phases.

### Calculator impact
The `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` set in `tariff-calculator.ts` was already correct (types 01/02/06 were already eligible, Type 09 still excluded). No logic change needed. However, the comments were Phase 1-only and didn't document Phase 2 scope or the critical filing sequence.

**What was updated:** `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`
- Expanded comment block above `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` to document both phases, Phase 2 scope, and the critical filing-sequence warning
- Added `CAPE_PHASE2_LAUNCH_DATE` exported constant (Jun 29, 2026) for use by UI components that may want to surface this to users

## 🟠 Background: Section 122 CIT Unconstitutionality Ruling (May 7, 2026)

The Court of International Trade ruled 2-1 on May 7, 2026 that Section 122 tariffs were unconstitutional. The government appealed to the Federal Circuit. The ruling did NOT stay collection pending appeal — tariffs continued to be collected through the statutory expiry on July 24, 2026. This is noted in the `S122-01` entry but does not change calculator eligibility logic.

**Source:** [Skadden: US Trade Court Strikes Down Section 122 Tariffs](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs)
